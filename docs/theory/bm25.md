# BM25

**Intuition.** BM25 scores how well a document matches a query by counting query terms in the
document, dampening the value of repeating a term, and normalizing by document length. Rare
query terms (e.g. a specific year or surname) matter more than common ones.

**Formula.** For query terms $q_1..q_n$ and document $d$:

$$\text{BM25}(d) = \sum_{i=1}^{n} \text{IDF}(q_i)\cdot
\frac{f(q_i, d)\,(k_1 + 1)}{f(q_i, d) + k_1\left(1 - b + b\,\frac{|d|}{\text{avgdl}}\right)}$$

with $f$ the term frequency, $|d|$ the document length, $\text{avgdl}$ the average length, and
the usual $k_1 \approx 1.5$, $b \approx 0.75$. `rank_bm25`'s `BM25Okapi` uses these parameters.

**Example.** For the claim *"Aspirin was synthesised by Felix Hoffmann in 1897"*, BM25 assigns
most of the score to documents containing "Aspirin", "Hoffmann" and "1897" — terms a dense model
can blur.

**In VerityAI.** `backend/app/retrieval/lexical.py` (`BM25Index`) is the lexical arm. It makes
exact names, dates, quantities and technical terms retrievable, and is fused with the dense arm.

**Limitations.** Pure lexical matching misses paraphrases ("the city of light" vs "Paris"), and
BM25 scores are unbounded, so they are never added directly to cosine similarities — RRF is used
instead.
